## 12. Answers to the scientific questions

1. **Is tone mapping stable within a shot?** — **Partially.** Typical low-luminance temporal differences are small, but high-end P90/P95/max differences grow substantially.
2. **Does the curve vary between shots?** — **Yes, quantitatively.** Variation rises from median `0.00972` nits at 1 nit to `1.60895` nits at 500 nits, with P95 `8.56675` nits at 500.
3. **Can part of a shot predict another part?** — **Partially.** 24 anchors completed both holdout splits with valid nonempty-region metrics, but 16 anchors lacked resolvable/long-enough shots and many requested tonal regions were legitimately empty.
4. **Is shot-level better than Model A?** — **Not universally.** It improved combined global MAE by `6.88%` and shadow MAE by `56.37%`, but worsened combined RMSE by `10.21%`, midtone MAE by `24.94%`, and BR2049 global MAE by `63.10%` relative to Model A.
5. **Does it improve high-end reproduction?** — **No for the measured ranges.** Matrix/combined 200–500 and 500–1000 nits were worse; >1000 nits was insufficient data.
6. **Does it reduce the P2.33 domain/clamping problem?** — **Yes, based on available support counts.** Outside-domain percentage fell from 35.77% to 7.24% for Matrix, 36.73% to 9.11% for BR2049, and 36.56% to 8.78% combined. Exact test min/max were not persisted.
7. **Does it generalize between Matrix and BR2049?** — **Partially and inconsistently.** Both materials produced valid anchors, but shot-level metrics improved Matrix global MAE while worsening BR2049 global MAE; BR2049 high-end data was unavailable. The geometry warning remains applicable.
8. **Is shot-adaptive mapping justified as the next production algorithm direction?** — **Conditionally for further validation, not production integration.** The shadow/domain evidence supports continued investigation, but high-end degradation, BR2049 generalization, unresolved shots, and the disk-budget failure require correction before production adoption.
